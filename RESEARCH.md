## 1. What details does an Indian HR team keep for every employee? Which are mandatory and which are sensitive?

**Typical record, grouped:**

| Group | Fields |
|---|---|
| Identity & personal | Full name, father's/spouse's name, date of birth, gender, marital status, blood group, nationality |
| Contact | Mobile, personal and work email, current and permanent address, **emergency contact** (name, relationship, phone) |
| Job | Employee code, date of joining, designation, department, location, reporting manager, grade, employment type, confirmation date, last working day, exit reason |
| Statutory | PAN, Aadhaar, UAN (EPF), ESIC insurance number (if covered) |
| Bank | Account number, IFSC, bank and branch name, account holder |
| Documents | Offer letter, ID/address/education proofs, medical certificates |

**What is "mandatory":** the law does not make every field mandatory *for the employee to give*; it makes them mandatory for the *employer to be able to do something*:

- **PAN** — needed for TDS and Form 16. Without PAN the employer must deduct TDS at a higher rate (Income-tax Act s.206AA) **[V]**. Treat as required before the first payroll.
- **UAN** — EPF number, 12 digits. Existing members keep their old UAN; for a first-time member the employer generates it. So it is **optional for new joiners** at the time of joining **[P]** and filled in once available.
- **Aadhaar** — the employer needs it mostly for EPF/UAN KYC. The Supreme Court (Puttaswamy, 2018) limited compulsory use of Aadhaar by private entities, so collect it where a legal purpose exists and keep it minimal **[V]**.
- **Bank account + IFSC** — required to pay salary by transfer.
- **Emergency contact** — not legally required but standard on a food plant (accident response).

**Sensitive (must be protected):** PAN, Aadhaar, bank details, date of birth, medical certificates, address/phone. Aadhaar is the most restricted: UIDAI guidance is to mask the first 8 digits on display and not to store or share it casually **[V]**. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 puts a duty on the employer to protect personal data and limit use to a stated purpose; its Rules came into force in phases from late 2025 **[V]**.

**Design consequences:** statutory and bank data live in **separate tables** with stricter row-level security than the employee row; display is masked (`ABCDE****F`, `XXXX XXXX 1234`, last 4 of account); revealing is logged; Aadhaar is never revealed [P] (see open question OQ-1 in ARCHITECTURE.md).

## 2. How is an employee code formed, and why should it never change?

Most companies use **fixed prefix + zero-padded running number**: `KCF0001`, `KCF0002`… (KCF = Konkan Coastal Foods). Some add the joining year (`KCF24-017`). We use the simple form and **do not encode attributes** such as department or location in the code, because those change on transfer and a code that stops being true is worse than no meaning at all.

**Why it must never change (and never be reused):**
1. It is the **business key** shared outside the system: the payroll vendor's file, PF/ESI records, ID cards, the monthly CSV, emails. Changing it breaks reconciliation with those.
2. **History hangs off it** — attendance, leave and audit entries. Reusing a code of a leaver for a new joiner would silently merge two people's histories.
3. People move departments, locations, managers and designations; the code is the one thing that stays stable.

So: generated by the database from a sequence (race-free, unique), immutable after insert, never reused, gaps are acceptable. Rejoining after exit is out of scope; reactivating the same record is the natural treatment **[V: confirm with HR]**.

## 3. Leave types in Indian companies; statutory entitlement vs company policy

| Type | Usual meaning |
|---|---|
| **CL** Casual Leave | Short, unplanned personal needs; usually 1–3 days at a time, no carry-forward |
| **SL** Sick Leave | Illness; medical certificate if longer than a few days; usually lapses |
| **EL / PL** Earned / Privilege Leave | Earned by service; planned long leave; carries forward; often encashable |
| **Maternity** | Statutory (Maternity Benefit Act) |
| **Paternity** | **No private-sector statute**; purely company policy (central government employees get 15 days) **[V]** |
| **LOP** Loss of Pay | Unpaid leave; used when paid balance is exhausted |
| **Comp-off** | Time off in lieu of work on a holiday/weekly off (**out of scope** here — no overtime/rosters) |

**Statutory entitlement vs company policy**

- A **statutory entitlement** is the *legal minimum* the employer cannot go below and cannot waive; it comes from a law that applies to the establishment (Factories Act for the plant, the Goa Shops & Establishments law for the office, Maternity Benefit Act for maternity). Disputes go to the labour authority.
- A **company policy** is what the employer chooses to give; it may *exceed* the statute but may never *fall below* it. Policy is contractual and can be changed prospectively (notice, consistency).
- Konkan's policy (CL 8, SL 7, EL 15, paternity 5) is policy **[P]**. Maternity is statutory. Our job is to make sure policy ≥ the statutory minimum for each location (§5).

**Maternity Benefit Act, 1961 (as amended 2017) [S]** — applies to establishments with 10 or more persons; Konkan qualifies.
- **26 weeks** paid leave for a woman with fewer than two surviving children (max 8 weeks before expected delivery); **12 weeks** (6 + 6) from the third child on.
- Must have **worked at least 80 days in the 12 months** before the expected delivery date.
- **12 weeks** for adopting mother (child under 3 months) and commissioning mother.
- Weekly offs/holidays falling inside the period **are counted** (calendar days) **[V]**.
- Crèche obligation for 50+ employees; work-from-home option after leave. (Not modelled.)
- The Act has been subsumed into the Code on Social Security, 2020 (in force 21 Nov 2025) per one secondary source **[V]**; the benefit terms were carried over.

→ **Consequence for the system:** Maternity (and Paternity) are *event-based*, not annual-accrual leave. Maternity counts **calendar days**, which is an exception to Konkan's "working days only" rule (§10 LR-12).

## 4. How does leave accrue? What happens to unused leave at year end?

Three common models:

| Model | How | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Upfront** | Full annual quota credited on 1 January (or financial-year start) | CL/SL |
| **Monthly** | A fixed amount credited per month (or per completed month) | EL/PL |
| **Pro-rated** | Joiners/leavers get quota × (part of year employed) | Mid-year joiners |

**Konkan [P]:** CL 8 and SL 7 are credited 1 Jan and pro-rated for joiners; EL accrues 1.25 days per **completed** month (12 × 1.25 = 15). Leave year = January–December.

**Year-end treatment of unused leave** (three options in practice):
- **Lapse** — gone on 31 Dec (CL, SL at Konkan).
- **Carry forward** — rolls into next year, usually with a cap (EL, up to **30 days** at Konkan **[P]**).
- **Encashment** — paid out in cash at year end or on exit. Under the Factories Act accumulated annual leave is capped (30 days) and unused leave is generally encashed or paid at discharge/resignation **[V]**. Konkan's brief does not mention encashment and payroll is external, so **encashment is out of scope**; the HRMS only exposes the closing EL balance so the payroll vendor can compute it.

**Pro-rating rule I propose (needs sign-off, not in brief)** — the brief only says "pro-rated for joiners". My rule (LR-3): months counted = months from the joining month to December, counting the joining month **only if the joining day is on or before the 15th**; entitlement = annual quota × months / 12, rounded to the nearest 0.5. A 1 July joiner gets 6/12: **CL 4.0, SL 3.5** — matching the brief's test ("joined 1 July sees pro-rated CL and SL").

**Why "available balance" should be calculated, not stored** (answers the brief's design question): balances change by accrual, approval, cancellation, retro-dated approvals, holiday additions that cancel leave days, and HR adjustments. A stored number must be updated in every one of those paths; one missed or racing update (two managers approving at once, a cancel racing an approve) makes it wrong *permanently* and nobody can tell why. A balance computed from the facts (quota rules + approved leave days + adjustments) cannot drift. See ARCHITECTURE.md §4.

## 5. Shops & Establishments law for Goa — and which law applies to Panaji vs Verna

### 5.1 Which regime applies where (important for Konkan's two sites)

| Site | Nature | Governing law |
|---|---|---|
| **Panaji head office** | Commercial establishment / office | **Goa Shops and Establishments Act, 1973** (Goa Act 13 of 1974 per the PRS PDF filename **[V]**) |
| **Verna plant** | Food-processing **factory** (power-driven, 10+ workers) | **Factories Act, 1948** — hours, weekly holiday, annual leave with wages. S&E Acts normally *exclude* factories **[V]** |

**Labour Codes (recent change) [S][V].** The **Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions (OSH) Code, 2020** was brought into force on **21 November 2025** (notification S.O. 5321(E)) and *replaces the Factories Act, 1948* among 13 central laws; Central Rules were notified on 8 May 2026 (G.S.R. 345(E)); until state rules are notified, existing rules continue to operate. Whether the OSH Code absorbs the **state** Shops Acts is not confirmed: the Code's list of 13 repealed laws did not obviously include them **[V]**. In addition, one secondary source reports a new **Goa Shops and Establishments (Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service) Act, 2025** notified 1 Oct 2025 with the commencement date not yet announced **[V]**.

> **Practical stance for this project:** implement Konkan's written policy [P]; use the old-Act figures below only as a *floor check*; and store the legal references in `policy_documents` so that a change in law is a data change, not a code change. State in the demo that both the Act text and its current in-force status were checked on a stated date (fill in §12).

### 5.2 Working hours, weekly off and leave

| Topic | Factories Act, 1948 (Verna plant) | Goa Shops & Establishments (Panaji office) |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly hours | **48 h** (s.51) **[S]** | **48 h** per the 2025 Act per a secondary source **[S][V]** |
| Daily hours | **9 h** (s.54), exceedable only with Chief Inspector approval **[S]** | **10 h** daily maximum per the same secondary source **[S][V]** |
| Rest interval | 30 min after 5 h continuous work (s.55); spread-over 10½ h (s.56) **[V]** | 30 min after 5 h **[S][V]** |
| Weekly off | One day per week, ordinarily Sunday (s.52); a *compensatory holiday* if the weekly holiday is lost **[S][V]** | ≥ 24 consecutive hours per week; compensatory off within 2 months if denied **[S][V]** |
| Overtime | Twice the ordinary rate (s.59) **[V]** | Twice the ordinary wage **[S][V]** |
| Annual leave | 1 day per 20 days worked once the worker has worked **240 days** in the previous calendar year; accumulation capped at 30 days (s.79) **[S: section exists; figures V]** | Not retrieved. One HR-vendor source says Goa gives a **combined 12-day CL+SL** entitlement **[V — commercial source; check statute]** |
| Under OSH Code | 1 day per 20 days worked, eligibility lowered to **180 days** **[V — my understanding]** | — |
| National/festival holidays | Check the state's *Industrial Establishments (National & Festival Holidays and Casual Leave)* enactment, if Goa has one **[V]** | Same |

**What this means for Konkan's policy:**
- **Plant:** Mon–Sat × 8 h = **48 h/week**, i.e. exactly at the weekly cap. Any extra hour is overtime (out of scope; flag in the demo). One weekly off (Sunday) satisfies the statute **[P]**.
- **Office:** Mon–Fri × 8 h = 40 h; two weekly offs (Sat, Sun) is *more generous* than the statute **[P]**.
- **Leave:** EL 15 days/year. A plant worker who works ~300 days earns 300 ÷ 20 = **15 days** under s.79 — Konkan's policy equals the statutory floor at the plant, so we must not reduce it **[V]**. CL 8 + SL 7 = 15 ≥ the (unverified) 12-day Goa minimum.
- The 8-hour "Present" threshold in the brief **[P]** is consistent with the 8-hour norm.

### 5.3 Sandwich rule (brief p.9 asks for this)

A **sandwich rule** says that if a weekly off or holiday falls *between* two days of leave, it is *also* counted as leave. Example, head office with a sandwich rule: leave Fri + Mon → Sat and Sun are sandwiched → **4 days** deducted. Employers use it to stop people stretching leave around weekends.

**Konkan has no sandwich rule [P]**: weekly offs and holidays inside a leave period are never counted. Head office Fri→Mon = **2 days**. Plant Fri→Mon = **3 days** (Fri, Sat, Mon; only Sunday is off). Our working-day function simply skips weekly offs and holidays; there is no "sandwich" branch. (Maternity is the exception, §3.)

## 6. National holiday vs restricted (optional) holiday vs state holiday

| Kind | Meaning | Who decides | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **National** | Observed across India; employers conventionally give all of them | Central govt | Republic Day 26 Jan, Independence Day 15 Aug, Gandhi Jayanti 2 Oct |
| **State** | Public holidays notified by the state govt for that state (festival/regional) | State govt (Goa: General Administration Dept) | Goa Liberation Day 19 Dec, Goa Statehood Day 30 May, Feast of St Francis Xavier 3 Dec, Christmas, Good Friday, Ganesh Chaturthi, Diwali, Dussehra **[V: confirm each date]** |
| **Restricted / Optional** | A *list* offered; each employee may take **a limited number** (commonly 2 per year) of their choice; everyone else works | Govt list / company policy | Regional or religious festivals |

Facts and cautions:
- Goa's 2026 public, restricted and special holidays were notified by the Goa Government (release dated 13 Oct 2025; notification No. 37/8/2025-GAD-III/4171 per secondary sites) **[S]**. Secondary sites **disagree on several dates** (Holi 3 vs 4 Mar; Bakrid 27 vs 28 May) because lunar-calendar dates are decided late. **Never hard-code dates; HR enters them from the official notification** (A7). I will seed the holiday list from the official PDF and cite it in the README.
- Holidays differ by **location** even within one company (the brief's Panaji vs Verna case: a Verna-only state holiday must not appear for Panaji).
- **Optional holiday treatment (my proposal, not in brief — OQ-3):** an Optional holiday is shown on the calendar but is a **working day** for everyone; it does not reduce working-day counts. An employee who wants it off applies CL for that date. This avoids inventing a leave type the policy table does not have.
- A holiday that falls on a weekly off is not an extra day off (no compensatory holiday in this system): shown as Weekly Off (LR-10).

## 7. Late arrivals, half days, work-from-home, missed punches

Norms (HR-vendor and company-policy practice **[V: general practice]**):

| Situation | Typical treatment |
|---|---|
| **Late arrival** | Grace window; after it a "late mark"; N late marks per month = half-day deduction (3 is very common) |
| **Half day** | Worked fewer than full hours but more than a minimum (e.g., 4 h); counts as 0.5 present |
| **WFH** | Needs manager approval or a flag; counts as present; some firms still check hours |
| **Missed punch** | Employee raises a **regularisation request** with a reason; manager approves; many firms cap how many per month or how far back |

**Konkan [P]:** ≥ 8 h Present; 4 to <8 h Half Day; <4 h or no punch Absent. Check-in **after 10:15** is Late; 3 Late marks in a calendar month = 0.5 day LOP. Employee marks WFH → counts as Present. Approved leave beats punch. Regularisation needs manager approval and changes attendance only then.

**Gaps the brief leaves, with my proposed defaults (all listed in ARCHITECTURE.md open questions):**
- **"After 10:15"**: late if time-of-day is strictly later than 10:15:00 IST (so 10:15:00 is on time, 10:15:01 is late).
- **Check-in but no check-out**: hours cannot be computed → day shows **Absent (missing check-out)** until regularised; HR warning list (AR-8). Harsh but consistent with "no punch = Absent", and it is why regularisation exists.
- **WFH**: a flag on the day's record, set at check-in; the day counts as **Present** regardless of hours; Late rule not applied (AR-7). Cannot be marked on weekly offs, holidays or leave days.
- **Half Day = 0.5 Present + 0.5 LOP** unless the other half is covered by approved half-day leave (AR-5). Needs HR confirmation because it changes payroll.
- **Regularisation window**: up to 30 days back (mirrors the leave back-dating limit).

### 10.1 Data validation (DV)

| ID | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| DV-1 | PAN: exactly 10 characters, `^[A-Z]{5}[0-9]{4}[A-Z]$` (input upper-cased). `ABC123` rejected. | Brief p.8 |
| DV-2 | Aadhaar: 12 digits `^[0-9]{12}$`. Stored; **never displayed unmasked**; display `XXXX XXXX 1234`. (Optional tightening: first digit 2–9 + Verhoeff checksum, app layer only.) | p.8 |
| DV-3 | UAN: 12 digits; optional. | p.8 |
| DV-4 | IFSC: `^[A-Z]{4}0[A-Z0-9]{6}$` (11 chars). | p.8 |
| DV-5 | Mobile: `^[6-9][0-9]{9}$`. A 9-digit number is rejected. | p.8 |
| DV-6 | PIN code: 6 digits `^[0-9]{6}$` (optional tightening: not starting with 0). State chosen from a list of 28 states + 8 UTs (ISO 3166-2:IN codes). | p.8 |
| DV-7 | Age at joining ≥ 18: `date_of_joining ≥ dob + 18 years` (complete years, leap-day safe). Age 17 rejected. | p.8 |
| DV-8 | Bank account: digits only, 9–18 digits **[V]**; displayed as last 4. | p.6 E7 |

### 10.2 Attendance (AR)

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| AR-1 | All times stored as exact instants and shown in IST; the attendance *day* is the IST calendar date of check-in; clock of the browser/server is never used (database `now()` is). |
| AR-2 | One attendance record per employee per IST date (unique). A second check-in the same day is rejected as a no-op, not a new row. One check-in and one check-out per day. |
| AR-3 | Weekly offs: Head office (Panaji) Sat+Sun; Plant (Verna) Sun. Held on the location record. A Saturday is a working day at the plant, a weekly off at head office. |
| AR-4 | Worked time = check-out − check-in. ≥ 8 h → Present; ≥ 4 h and < 8 h → Half Day; < 4 h or no punch → Absent. |
| AR-5 | Half Day = 0.5 Present + 0.5 LOP, unless the other half is approved half-day leave (then 0.5 Present + 0.5 On Leave). *(proposed)* |
| AR-6 | **Late**: check-in strictly after 10:15:00 IST on a working day. Every full group of **3 Late marks in a calendar month** converts to **0.5 day LOP** (3→0.5, 6→1.0, …). The LOP is subtracted from Present, so totals still reconcile. Late marks on days that end up On Leave/Holiday/Weekly Off are not counted. |
| AR-7 | WFH: flag set by the employee; day counts as Present; hours and Late rules not applied; shown separately as WFH days in the CSV. Not allowed on weekly off, holiday, or an approved-leave day. *(proposed)* |
| AR-8 | Check-in without check-out → Absent (missing check-out) until regularised; appears on HR's month-end warning list. *(proposed)* |
| AR-9 | **Day-status precedence** (first match wins): outside employment (before joining / after last working day) → *not applicable*; approved **full-day leave** → On Leave (even if punched in); **Weekly Off**; **Holiday** (National or State; **Optional is not a holiday**); otherwise punch-derived Present/Half Day/Absent (WFH → Present). Future dates have no status; today is provisional until the day ends. Approved half-day leave combines with the punch-derived status of the other half. |
| AR-10 | Punches on a Weekly Off/Holiday are stored but do not change that day's status (no comp-off in scope); flagged "worked on off day" for HR. |
| AR-11 | Regularisation: employee requests a correction for a day within 30 days with a reason; manager (not self) approves/rejects with comment; **attendance changes only on approval**; one pending request per employee per day. HR may correct directly but a reason is mandatory and it is audited. |
| AR-12 | Holidays are per location. A Verna-only holiday does not exist for Panaji. Adding a holiday on a date where an employee at that location has an approved leave day releases that leave day (balance restored) and notifies the employee (LR-11). |

### 10.3 Leave (LR)

Policy table **[P]** (brief p.8):

| Type | Quota | Accrual | Carry forward | Half day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CL | 8 | Credited 1 Jan; pro-rated for joiners | Lapses 31 Dec | Yes |
| SL | 7 | Credited 1 Jan; pro-rated for joiners | Lapses 31 Dec | Yes |
| EL | 15 | 1.25 per completed month | Up to 30 days | No |
| Maternity | Statutory (§3) | On request | n/a | No |
| Paternity | 5 days (policy) | On request | n/a | No |
| LOP | Unlimited | n/a | n/a | Yes |

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| LR-1 | Leave year = 1 January–31 December. |
| LR-2 | **Available = opening + accrued + adjustments − used (approved) .** Pending requests do **not** change the displayed balance; they are shown in a separate Pending column. |
| LR-3 | CL, SL accrued = quota credited on 1 Jan; for joiners: quota × months/12 (joining month counted only if joined on or before the 15th), rounded to nearest 0.5. Joined 1 Jul → CL 4.0, SL 3.5. *(rounding proposed)* |
| LR-4 | EL accrued = 1.25 × **completed** months of service in the year (joiner on 1 Jul has 1.25 on 1 Aug). Opening EL for a year = min(30, closing EL of the previous year). 30 is a year-end carry-forward limit, not a ceiling on in-year balance. *(interpretation)* |
| LR-5 | CL/SL carry forward nothing (opening = 0 each year). |
| LR-6 | **Working days** counted for a leave request = dates in range that are not the location's weekly off and not a National/State holiday. **No sandwich rule.** Head office Fri→Mon = 2; Plant Fri→Mon = 3. |
| LR-7 | Half day only for types that allow it (CL, SL, LOP); half-day request is a single date → 0.5. |
| LR-8 | **Insufficient balance:** an employee cannot submit leave beyond *(available − pending of the same type)*. The app offers to convert the **excess** to LOP; the request is stored with the paid part on the chosen type and the excess as LOP (per day). Requests for LOP itself are always allowed. |
| LR-9 | **Sick Leave of more than 2 consecutive days** (i.e. ≥ 3 leave days in one request) cannot be submitted without a medical certificate upload. |
| LR-10 | Weekly offs and holidays inside the range are never counted as leave. A holiday on a weekly off shows as Weekly Off. |
| LR-11 | If a holiday is added (or a weekly off changes) over an approved/pending leave day, that day is released from the request and the balance restored automatically; the employee is notified. |
| LR-12 | **Maternity** counts **calendar days** (statute), max 26 weeks (182 days) per event, 12 weeks (84 days) from the third child — HR verifies the child count on the certificate; requires document; female employees only; ≥ 80 days service in last 12 months. **Paternity**: 5 working days per event (policy), male employees only. Both on request, not in the annual balance; manager approves, HR sees all. |
| LR-13 | **No back-dating beyond 30 days:** `from_date ≥ today(IST) − 30`. Future dates are allowed. |
| LR-14 | Overlapping leave: an employee cannot have two active (pending/approved) requests covering the same date. |
| LR-15 | **Approval:** only the employee's **direct manager** or **any HR Admin**, never the employee themselves. Reject/approve takes a comment. Balance changes **only on approval**. The balance is re-checked at approval time; if it is no longer sufficient, approval is refused. |
| LR-16 | **Cancel:** employee may cancel a pending request, or an approved one whose first day has not started (today(IST) < from_date). Balance is restored. Started leave cannot be cancelled by the employee (HR can adjust with a reason). |
| LR-17 | Email: manager is emailed on a new request; the employee is emailed on approval or rejection. |
| LR-18 | Month-end summary per employee: days present (incl. 0.5s, net of late-LOP), WFH days, leave days by type, LOP days (= approved LOP leave + Absent days + 0.5 × half-day absences + late-LOP), holidays, weekly offs. **Reconciliation invariant:** present + leave + LOP + holidays + weekly offs = days employed in that month (mid-month joiners/leavers are counted only for employed days). CSV totals must match the calendar. |
| LR-19 | Employees without a reporting manager (top of hierarchy) have leave approved by an HR Admin; an HR Admin's own leave goes to their manager. *(proposed)* |
